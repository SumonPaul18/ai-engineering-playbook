## Writing Requirements

Write the post in **simple but professional English** suitable for an international LinkedIn audience.

The post must feel like a **real engineer sharing an actual technical experience**, not like an AI-generated tutorial or marketing advertisement.

### Required Structure

**1. Strong Hook**
Start with a short and technically relevant hook based on:

* a real infrastructure problem,
* a current industry requirement,
* a practical engineering challenge,
* or an interesting observation.

Do NOT start with generic phrases such as:

* "I am excited to share..."
* "Recently, I had the opportunity..."
* "Today I want to share..."
* "This was an amazing learning experience..."

**2. Real Scenario**
Briefly explain why I started the R&D and what real-world problem or engineering requirement I was trying to understand or solve.

**3. Hands-on Story**
Describe my actual R&D journey naturally:

* environment preparation
* installation/configuration
* integration
* testing
* troubleshooting
* validation
* failure/recovery scenarios

Do not make it sound like a step-by-step tutorial.
Focus on the engineering journey rather than listing commands.

**4. Technical Depth**
Mention important technical components naturally.
Use technical terms in English:
Kubernetes, OpenStack, Ceph, Terraform, IaC, API, OSD, RBD, automation, networking, authentication, etc.

Do not over-explain basic concepts.

**5. Real-Time Engineering Situation**
Include at least one realistic problem → investigation → solution/validation sequence.

Show that failures and errors were part of the R&D process.

**6. Avoid Generic Personal Reflection**
Do NOT end with:

* "What I learned..."
* "This journey taught me..."
* "My biggest takeaway..."
* "I realized..."

Instead, connect the experience to **current infrastructure engineering needs**.

**7. Industry / Engineering Hook at the End**
The ending should explain why this technology or approach is important today.

Use this concept:

"Modern infrastructure is becoming more complex, and repeatedly provisioning infrastructure manually creates operational risk, inconsistency and configuration drift. Infrastructure Automation is therefore becoming an engineering necessity—not simply a way to save time."

Also connect it with modern automation tools where relevant:

"Terraform, OpenTofu, Ansible and similar tools help make infrastructure provisioning more repeatable, predictable, consistent, reproducible and manageable."

If the topic is relevant to AI, connect it naturally:

"AI is accelerating application development and digital services, but every AI workload still depends on infrastructure underneath it. That infrastructure also needs to be automated, scalable and reproducible."

Do NOT exaggerate or claim that AI "does everything."

**8. Career / Professional Positioning**
Subtly position me as an engineer who:

* works hands-on with infrastructure,
* validates technology through R&D,
* troubleshoots real problems,
* thinks about automation and scalability,
* and connects technical implementation with real-world engineering requirements.

Do NOT explicitly say:
"I am a professional engineer"
or
"This proves my expertise."

Let the experience demonstrate it.

**9. GitHub Resource**
Add the GitHub link naturally at the end:

📘 Full Practical Guide:
🔗 [GITHUB LINK]

**10. Hashtags**
Use 8–12 relevant hashtags.
Include a mix of:

* technology
* engineering
* DevOps / Cloud
* Infrastructure
* Open Source
* R&D / Home Lab

Avoid irrelevant or excessive hashtags.

## Style

* Humanized
* Professional
* Technical
* Engineer-focused
* Real-scenario based
* Practical storytelling
* Concise
* Easy to read on LinkedIn
* No corporate buzzword overload
* No exaggerated claims
* No fake achievements
* No generic motivational language

Target length: **8–15 short lines/paragraphs**, depending on the technical depth.

The final result must be **copy-paste ready for LinkedIn**.
